Find what you're seeing below. Most problems show a clear message in the console, and these sections use the same words. If something seems down for everyone, check [System status](/support/system-status) first.

## Sign-in and access

| You see | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Please sign in again** | Your session ended. | Sign in again. You come back to where you were. |
| **Check your inbox** | Your email isn't confirmed. | Open the link we sent, then choose **I've confirmed it**. It can take a minute to arrive. |
| **This account can't sign in here** | Your sign-in worked, but this account isn't allowed in. | Sign in with the account your team invited. |
| **This invite has expired** | The invite ran out. | Ask whoever invited you for a new link. |
| **This invite is for someone else** | You're signed in with a different address. | Choose **Switch account** and use the invited address. |
| **You don't have access to this** | Your account can't open this in your organization. | Ask an admin. If you're in more than one organization, check you're in the right one. |
| **Can't reach RubixKube** | Your connection dropped, or we can't be reached. | Check you're online and try again. What's on screen stays there. |
| **RubixKube is having trouble** | Something on our side failed. | Try again in a moment. If it keeps happening, write to us. |

## Sources

### An observer doesn't report in

The connect dialog waits for the observer's first report. It usually arrives within a minute. If the dialog says **Taking a little longer**, check that the command finished without errors.

On Kubernetes, check the pod and read its logs:

```bash
kubectl get pods -n rubixkube-system
kubectl logs deploy/rubixkube-observer -n rubixkube-system
```

On a server, use `sudo systemctl status rk-observer` on Linux. On AWS or Google Cloud installs in Docker, use `sudo docker logs rk-observer`.

Then check three things. The machine needs outbound access to `api.rubixkube.ai:443` and `nats.rubixkube.ai:4222`. On Kubernetes, the node needs to be able to pull `ghcr.io/rubixkube-io/observer-agent`. And the key needs to be the one from your organization's connect dialog.

### A source says Disconnected

A source reads **Disconnected** after 5 minutes with no heartbeat. Use the checks above. If the machine was stopped or the observer was uninstalled, start it again or reinstall it.

### A source says Needs attention

Open the source. The page lists what's wrong under **The observer needs attention**. For an observer on a big cluster, it also tells you how many pods to run.

### A tool isn't sending anything

The dialog says **Waiting for the first message** until the tool sends something. Check these in order:

1. The webhook URL is in the place the dialog named, in the tool itself.
2. The Datadog or Observe webhook has the payload from the dialog. Those two send whatever template they're given.
3. The automation is on. If the dialog said **The automation wasn't installed**, choose **Try again**.
4. Grafana's contact point has **Max alerts per message** set to 1.

The URL is shown only once. If you lost it, delete the source and connect it again.

## Incidents and RCAs

### The RCA hasn't started

This is usually on purpose. The incident says what it's waiting for:

| You see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **RCA starts in about N min** | The wait by severity. Critical has none, high is 2 minutes, medium 5 and low 10. Observers also hold an incident for up to 10 minutes to confirm it, unless it's critical. |
| **Triaging** | Triage runs first, and the RCA follows. |
| **Verifying** | It looks healthy again. Rubix is checking that it holds. |
| **In a group** | The RCA runs on the group. Open the group to see it. |
| **Queued** | Other RCAs are running first. |
| **Retrying** | An attempt failed, and Rubix is trying again. |
| **Waiting for RCA service** | The RCA service can't be reached. Rubix keeps trying and starts when it's back. |

A normal wait can't be skipped from the console. **Run the RCA again** shows once an RCA has finished or failed, or when none is coming. To change the waits for everyone, use **Settings > Incidents**.

### No RCA is coming

Some incidents don't get an RCA, and the page says why:

- **It recovered before the RCA started, so no RCA runs.** If it breaks again, the RCA starts.
- **Earlier RCAs say this is expected behaviour, so no RCA runs.** This is a Known alert. Choose **Ignore**, or **Run RCA again** if it's wrong.
- **This keeps coming back.** Rubix has investigated it as often as your settings allow this week. Snooze it, ignore it or run the RCA again. You can change the limit in **Settings > Incidents**.
- **The RCA was due and has not started**, or **The last RCA attempt stopped.** Run the RCA again, or ask Rubix what it can see.

### The RCA failed

The incident says why, and when the next automatic try is. An RCA gets up to three attempts.

| Reason shown | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The AI model was rate limited by its provider. | Wait for the next try. If you use your own model, check its quota in **Settings > Models**. |
| The AI model the RCA uses was not available. | Check the model in **Settings > Models**. |
| The RCA ran past its time limit. | Run it again. |
| The RCA service could not be reached. | Check [System status](/support/system-status). |

### The RCA says Not determined

Rubix didn't have enough to state a cause, and it says so. This often means it couldn't read something it needed. Check **Customize > Integrations** for what's connected, add a comment with what you know, and use **Run RCA again**.

## Rubix

- **Rubix won't change anything.** The chat is in Observe mode. Change the mode in the chat, up to the highest mode in **Settings > Modes**.
- **You can't pick a higher mode.** An admin sets the highest mode anyone can use. See [Modes](/using/modes).
- **Rubix stopped and nothing's happening.** In Assist, it's waiting for you. Look for the approval card in the chat.
- **Rubix can't see a tool.** Open the integration under **Customize > Integrations**. Its **Reads** section shows what the connection can read. **Needs attention** means the connection has an error.

## Slack

- **Slack comes with Business.** Slack needs a Business or Enterprise plan.
- **Slack needs new permissions.** An admin chooses **Reconnect** in **Customize > Channels**.
- **Messages go to the wrong channel.** Set a channel for each category in **Customize > Channels**. A category with no channel goes with Incidents.

## MCP

Your AI tool signs in with your own RubixKube account, so sign in when it asks. The server is `https://mcp.rubixkube.ai/mcp`. See [MCP](/integrations/mcp).

## Plans and limits

A card that says something like **Automations come with Business** means your plan doesn't include that feature. **Settings > Usage and plan** shows your limits and marks each one **Near limit**, **At limit** or **Over limit**.

## Before you write to us

Choose **Help and support** in your user menu, or write to connect@rubixkube.ai. These help us answer faster:

- Your Organization ID, from **Settings > General**
- The incident code, such as INC-12, or the source name
- The time it happened, with your timezone
- What you saw, what you expected, and a screenshot
- For an observer, the output of the `kubectl` commands above. Remove any secrets first.
