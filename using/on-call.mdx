On-Call connects incoming alerts to the people responsible for a service. A service owns an escalation policy; the policy can target responders directly or resolve a schedule when a page is due. Incidents, pages, and handoffs stay visible in the console.

On-Call must be enabled for your tenant before its pages appear. A tenant admin may need a RubixKube superadmin to enable it. A cluster is not required for webhook alerting, although current first-run onboarding may still direct new workspaces to environment setup. SMS, voice, native push, and the mobile app are unavailable. Email delivery depends on relay setup; in-app delivery uses the Console event stream. See [capabilities and availability](/using/on-call-capabilities) for current limits.

## Set up a service

1. Open **On-Call → Teams** and create the team that owns the service. Add responders and choose their roles.
2. Open **On-Call → Schedules** and create a rotation. Add participants, choose its timezone and handoff time, and inspect the coverage preview. Use overrides for planned absences.
3. Open **On-Call → Policies** and create ordered escalation steps. Each step can target a person or schedule and has a delay before the next step. Save the policy and use its resolve preview to check who would be paged now.
4. Open **On-Call → Services**, create the service, and select its team and escalation policy. Configure routing, urgency and maintenance rules on the service page.
5. In the service's **Integrations** tab, add Alertmanager or a generic webhook. Save the integration key when it is shown: it cannot be retrieved later. Use **Test** to send a sample alert through the ingest path and verify the resulting incident.

## Prepare your own notifications

Open **On-Call → My On-Call → Manage notification settings**. Add a reachable contact method, choose your timezone, set reminders and channel rules, then send a personal test page. Only configured channels can be selected. If your organization has not configured a provider, its channel may be unavailable even when your profile contains a contact method.

SMS and voice providers are not currently available, so adding or verifying a phone does not enable those delivery channels. The delivery ledger shows what Pager attempted and why a channel was skipped.

## Work an incident

The **Incidents** list filters by status, urgency, service, team and ownership. Open an incident to see its alerts, timeline, assigned responders, escalation steps and notification ledger. Acknowledge to take responsibility, add an operational note, reassign or escalate when another responder is needed, and resolve when the issue is addressed. Resolving also resolves the incident's open alerts.

**My On-Call** shows your current and next coverage, assigned incidents and a test-page entry point. **Now** shows current level-zero coverage for active services; `?kiosk=1` gives the board a full-screen view.

## Swaps and handoffs

Request a swap from a future shift on its schedule page. The proposed recipient can accept or decline before the shift begins. A schedule handoff asks the outgoing responder for an optional note shortly before the boundary, then shows a summary to the incoming responder. Review and acknowledge a delivered handoff from the schedule or **My On-Call**.

## Operational checks

- If an alert creates no incident, inspect the service integration, routing rules, maintenance windows and event rules. The generic webhook mapping preview can identify a missing title or dedup key before sending.
- If a page is skipped, inspect the incident's **Notifications** panel and the responder's notification settings. A verified phone or linked chat identity may be required.
- If coverage is empty, inspect the schedule preview, overrides and escalation policy targets. The **Now** board shows the resulting coverage.

On-Call pages are separate from [general RubixKube notifications](/using/notifications), which distribute insights, reports and action updates.
